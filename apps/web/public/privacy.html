Ommesh privacy policy

Updated September 28, 2026.

Ommesh is a companion for MeshCore radios maintained by cm4ker and the Ommesh project. This policy describes the data handled by the app. No Ommesh account is required.

Data on your device

The app stores conversations, message drafts, radio contacts (not your phone's address book), public node identifiers, routes, positions supplied by radios, telemetry history, connection addresses and preferences locally. Map and elevation tiles are cached for reuse offline. Message history is separate for each radio.

When you choose to remember a node password, mobile builds use the platform's secure storage; desktop builds use the system credential store. Browser builds use the browser's local storage. Conversation history and cached map data are not separately encrypted by the app. The Android app excludes its private data from Android cloud backup and device-transfer backup.

Data sent to your radio and other people

Connecting sends companion-protocol commands to the radio you select over Bluetooth or Wi-Fi/TCP. Messages, channel settings, node names, passwords for remote administration and positions you set can be transmitted to that radio to perform the actions you request. The radio may forward messages, advertisements, positions and telemetry to other radios according to its settings. Anyone with access to a channel may be able to read that channel's messages.

Wi-Fi/TCP connections to companion radios do not use TLS. Use a network you trust. Radio encryption depends on the firmware, message type and channel keys; this app does not promise that all radio traffic is encrypted or that it can erase data received by other people.

Maps and elevation services

Opening the map or a terrain profile can request images from OpenStreetMap tile services and the public elevation-tiles-prod dataset hosted on Amazon Web Services. Those services receive your IP address, request time, client information and the coordinates of the requested map tiles. A viewed area may reveal an area of interest or a radio's location. These HTTPS requests do not include your conversations, node passwords or radio contact list. Provider logs and retention are governed by the providers' policies.

Coverage surveys

A coverage survey, which you start from "Who hears me" on the map, reads the phone's precise position while it runs and the app is open. At each point it records the time, the position and the repeaters that answered, with their signal. Surveys stay on your device until you delete them, or export them to a file whose destination you choose.

If you choose to send a survey to a community coverage map, the app asks each time, then sends its points (time, position, the repeater heard best at each point and its signal, and the app's name and version) to that site. The only such site at present is MeshCoreTel (meshcoretel.ru), run by a third party, not by the Ommesh project. The points become part of its public coverage map, visible to anyone; the site's own policies govern how it keeps them, and they cannot be deleted from the app afterwards.

Permissions

  • Nearby devices / Bluetooth: finding and connecting to a compatible radio.
  • Location: on Android 11 and earlier, required by Android for Bluetooth scanning. The phone's position is read only for something you turn on: "Where am I" on the map, using the device's position for a radio, following the phone with a radio, or a coverage survey. It is read while the app is open, never in the background. Radio positions may also be received from the mesh or entered manually.
  • Notifications: optional local notices for messages and discovered nodes. A notice can show a contact name and message preview on your lock screen, subject to your device settings.
  • Network access: connecting to a Wi-Fi radio and loading map or elevation tiles.

You can change permissions in your device settings. Denying Bluetooth access prevents Bluetooth connections; demo mode does not require a radio.

Analytics, advertising and updates

The app contains no advertising SDK, analytics SDK or automatic crash-reporting service. The project does not operate an account or message-storage server. Google Play may process installation and diagnostic information according to Google's settings and policies. Desktop builds can also contact GitHub for update checks and downloads; these requests expose the IP address and normal request information to GitHub. Android updates from Google Play are distributed by the store. Android development builds installed from GitHub ask GitHub whether a newer development build is out, which likewise exposes the IP address and normal request information.

Retention and deletion

Local history remains until you delete it or clear the app's storage. In the app, use Radio → Reboot, reset, erase → Delete the history on this device to remove the selected radio's conversation history. Saved node passwords can be forgotten in the node's sign-in controls. On Android, Settings → Apps → Ommesh → Storage → Clear storage removes the app's local history, settings, cached tiles and remembered passwords. Uninstalling also removes its app data.

Deleting local history does not delete data stored on a radio, copies held by recipients, or logs retained by map providers. Manage the radio's data separately. No app account needs to be deleted because the app does not create one.

Contact and changes

For privacy questions, email support@it-adapt.ru. You can also reach the maintainer through the Ommesh project support page. GitHub issues are public: do not post passwords, private messages or precise private locations there. The date above changes when this policy is updated.
